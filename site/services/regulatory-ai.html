[ The problem ]

Most AI tools flatten layered rules into mush.

Regulated bodies live with layered rules: a national standard, a state variation, a local exception, each overriding the one beneath it. Flatten them and you get confident, wrong answers.

Problem 1

The hierarchy gets ignored

Generic AI tools treat all rules as equal. Local exceptions and national defaults get mixed together. The answer might be technically correct for some context — just not yours.

Problem 2

Answers aren't cited

You can't verify what you can't trace. When there's no clause reference attached to an answer, there's no way to know if the ruling is right — or where it came from.

Problem 3

Context-blind answers

The same question can have different answers depending on grade, format, or jurisdiction. A tool that doesn't know which context it's answering for is guessing — and won't tell you it is.

The difference

We model the hierarchy, clause by clause.

The answer you get is the rule that actually governs your situation — not the closest match in a flattened corpus. And you can see exactly what it overrode to get there.

[ How it works ]

Three things that make the answers trustworthy.

[ 01 ]

Verbatim document ingestion

We extract your rulebook word-for-word into a structured, citeable corpus — every clause, table, and figure preserved exactly. This becomes the single source of truth the assistant is allowed to answer from. The AI isn't trained on the rules — it reads your actual documents.

[ 02 ]

Context-aware retrieval that respects the rule hierarchy

When a question comes in, the assistant pulls only the clauses that apply to that situation — and where a local rule overrides a national one, the local rule wins. The answer is grounded in those exact clauses and cited back to them.

[ 03 ]

Answers scoped to the asker's context

Rules differ by grade, format, and jurisdiction. Before answering, the assistant confirms the relevant context — asking a clarifying question if needed, exactly as an experienced administrator would. It only answers once it's confident it has the right scope.

[ Who it's for ]

The pattern: large rules corpus, many people who need to interpret it, cost when they get it wrong.

If that describes your organisation, there's a product here.

Sports Governing Bodies

Volunteers, officials, and administrators are fielding rules questions they don't always know the answer to. When they get it wrong, it's a protest, a replayed game, or a dispute that takes weeks to resolve. State and national rule layers make it worse — the right answer depends on which competition and which grade.

eg. MCC Laws → state playing conditions → local by-laws

Licensing & Regulatory Bodies

When a licensed professional applies the wrong standard for their jurisdiction, the downstream cost is real — fines, liability, re-certification, or voided work. Federal, state, and local rules stack on top of each other, and the answer isn't always obvious. Most tools ignore the hierarchy entirely.

eg. federal standard → state code → local variation

Professional Associations

Members call the office because they can't find the answer in the rulebook — or worse, they find the wrong one. CPD requirements, eligibility rules, and registration conditions vary by member category, year, and chapter. Staff answer the same questions hundreds of times a year.

eg. national rules → chapter conditions → member category
