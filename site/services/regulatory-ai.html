[ The problem ]

Most AI tools flatten layered rules into mush.

Regulated bodies live with layered rules: a national standard, a state variation, a local exception, each overriding the one beneath it. Flatten them and you get confident, wrong answers.

Problem 1

The hierarchy gets ignored

Generic AI tools treat all rules as equal. Local exceptions and national defaults get mixed together. The answer might be technically correct for some context — just not yours.

Problem 2

Answers aren't cited

You can't verify what you can't trace. When there's no clause reference attached to an answer, there's no way to know if the ruling is right — or where it came from.

Problem 3

Context-blind answers

The same question can have different answers depending on grade, format, or jurisdiction. A tool that doesn't know which context it's answering for is guessing — and won't tell you it is.

The difference

We model the hierarchy, clause by clause.

The answer you get is the rule that actually governs your situation — not the closest match in a flattened corpus. And you can see exactly what it overrode to get there.

[ What makes it different ]

Not a chatbot. Not document search. A compliance architecture.

The market is full of tools that let you ask questions about your documents. Most are a thin AI layer over basic keyword search. They don't understand document authority, can't resolve a conflict between two policies, and have no memory of how a question has been answered before.

01 — Rule Hierarchy

Understands which document overrides which

The assistant knows the priority of every document in your corpus: legislation sits above internal policy, policy above procedure, procedure above guidance. When rules conflict it doesn't surface the conflict and stop — it resolves it, citing which document governs and why.

02 — Citation Integrity

Every answer cites the exact clause, version, and document

Not a link to a document. Not "see Policy X." The exact section reference: "Section 4.2.1(b) of the Credit Compliance Framework, version June 2025." Every answer is auditable. Every decision is traceable to its source.

03 — Precedent Layer

Prior rulings surface alongside every answer

Every interpretation decision is logged. When the same question comes up again — this year, next year, after staff turnover — the prior ruling surfaces alongside the live rule. Institutional knowledge stays with the organisation, not with the person who left.

04 — Escalation Workflow

A documented path for the calls that genuinely need a human

When a question exceeds the tool's authority, one tap sends a formal referral with question, AI interpretation, cited clause, and context pre-loaded. The escalation is on record. The human who decides gets everything they need before the conversation starts.

Any organisation where rules are documented but the people applying them aren't compliance experts.

The same architecture works across industries because the problem is the same: layered documents, frontline staff applying them under pressure, and a compliance team that becomes the bottleneck.

CCO · Chief Compliance Officer · Registered Investment Adviser

Loan officers, advisers, and brokers interpret overlapping federal and state rules at the point of sale. The compliance team learns about misapplication when the CFPB or state regulator is already involved. Enforcement appetite across lending, disclosure, and fair lending has intensified — UDAAP violations and redlining patterns are among priority targets.

Healthcare & Aged Care

Director of Nursing · Chief Quality Officer · Medicare/Medicaid Provider CEO

25–35% staff turnover annually means the interpretation problem resets every season. Directors of Nursing field protocol questions while running the floor. CMS billing rules and coverage criteria update frequently — claiming errors are common and expensive. Survey visits surface misapplication that has been accumulating for years.

CMS false claims: $13,000–$26,000 per claim under the False Claims Act. OSHA willful violation: up to $156,259. CMS decertification halts all Medicare/Medicaid reimbursement.

Construction & Safety

National Safety Manager · HSE Manager · Site Manager

Distributed workforce, high subcontractor turnover, and licensing questions that are jurisdiction-specific. Updated OSHA silica and heat illness rules changed obligations across the sector and most businesses are still catching up. Safety officers spend significant time on "can this person do this work" questions that should be self-serve.

OSHA willful violation: up to $156,259 per violation. A single recordable fatality: $1M+ average total cost. Licensing non-compliance on a federal or state tender can void the contract.

Professional Associations

CEO · Director of Member Services · Registrar

Member services teams field thousands of "do I comply?" questions by phone and email. The wrong answer means a member practices with a lapsed licence, fails their CPD requirement, or breaches a condition they didn't know existed. The member carries the liability — the association carries the reputational risk.

Practising without a valid licence: criminal offence in most US professions. A state board investigation for a member: $30–150K in legal fees, public disciplinary record, income loss.
